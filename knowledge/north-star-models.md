## 1. Michael Gervais — Finding Mastery

**Who:** A high-performance psychologist who has worked across sport, business, the arts and science. Host of the *Finding Mastery* podcast and founder of Finding Mastery, a high-performance psychology consultancy built on **mindset training**. Author of *The First Rule of Mastery: Stop Worrying About What People Think of You* (Harvard Business Review Press, Nov 2023).

**Core ideas to learn from:**
- **FOPO, the fear of other people's opinions.** The key to a high-performance life is redirecting attention from the world outside us to the world inside us.
- **Mindset is trainable:** awareness practices like mindfulness, meditation, journaling and conversations with wise people. (This matches CMH's "your mind responds to reps.")
- **Mastery is a practice, not a destination.** The focus is the process of becoming, not the trophy.
- **Long-form, curious conversations with masters** across sport, business, the arts and high-stakes fields. This is almost exactly CMH's four guest corners (Ring, Arena, Studio, Line).
- A recurring push for people to **articulate their personal philosophy**: who you are and how you want to live, in a few words.

**What CMH borrows:**
| Program | Borrow |
|---|---|
| Podcast | Finding Mastery-level curiosity and depth. Ask about the *inner* game, not the highlight reel. Add a signature question: **"What's your personal philosophy?"** |
| Skool | A **FOPO course for fighters and high performers** (crowds, judges, social media, family expectations). Mindset-training language. A "Write Your Personal Philosophy" tool. |
| Ambassadors | Train athletes to perform from the inside out. The content kit includes inner-game prompts, not just hype. |
| Brian's voice | Calm, curious, unhurried. Asks the next question. Treats the guest as a master of their own life. |

**Don't copy:** clinical or consulting claims (CMH is a nonprofit education hub, and mental health care goes to Thrive) · his brand language as our headlines · any implied affiliation.

## 2. Arthur Brooks — From Strength to Strength

**Who:** A social scientist and Harvard professor who teaches the science of happiness. Writes *How to Build a Life* for *The Atlantic*. Author of *From Strength to Strength* (2022) and *Build the Life You Want* (2023, with Oprah Winfrey).

**Core ideas to learn from:**
- **Fluid vs. crystallized intelligence, or "the second curve."** Fluid intelligence (learning fast, solving new problems) peaks early. Crystallized intelligence (wisdom, experience, teaching) grows later. People who cling to the first curve suffer. People who jump to the second can flourish.
- **The striver's curse:** high achievers tie their worth to performance and suffer when it fades.
- **Happiness has three components: enjoyment, satisfaction and meaning/purpose.** You can't control them perfectly, but you can control them far more than most people think.
- **Satisfaction has two levers:** what you have and what you want. Managing your *wants* matters as much as getting more.
- **Happiness is teachable:** a skill set, taught like a Harvard course and written about like a weekly column. It's science made personal, practical and warm.
- The happiness "pillars" he writes about: **faith or life philosophy, family, friendship and meaningful work.** (This maps onto CMH's Community pillar.)

**What CMH borrows:**
| Program | Borrow |
|---|---|
| Newsletter | **The column model.** Like *How to Build a Life*: one human question a week, answered with one cited idea, a personal story and a practice. That's *Between Rounds*. |
| Skool | Courses that teach well-being as a **skill**. Course candidates: **"The Second Curve: Life After the Ring"** and **"Your Happiness Macros"** (enjoyment, satisfaction, meaning). |
| Podcast | A Brooks question in the Final Bell: **"Who are you when you're not competing?"** |
| Ambassadors / Thrive | Fighter careers are short, so the second curve is *the* fighter problem: identity after a loss, after an injury, after retirement. The post-fight check-in is CMH's answer to the striver's curse. |
| Audience reach | Brooks speaks to midlife high performers, which is Ring 4 of the audience map (business, art and service). It's a natural bridge from fighters to everyone. |

**Don't copy:** his politics or think-tank past · academic density (stay accessible) · his phrases as our brand lines.

## 2.5 Audience direction (founder decision, Oct 2026)
Anthony wants CMH to reach **the Gervais and Brooks audiences themselves**: high performers across business, sport, the arts and service, and people seeking happiness and meaning. So we borrow these two for **audience and register** as well as method:
- **Register:** intelligent, curious, warm, research-informed, accessible. Fighter language is the *flavor*, not the dialect.
- **Titles:** universal first, with the fighter story inside. ("Performing When Everyone's Watching," not "FOPO for Fighters.")
- **Guests:** go well beyond combat sports. Aim for roughly half from the Arena/Studio/Line corners, plus researchers and authors in the Gervais/Brooks orbit.
- **Platforms that reach these audiences:** YouTube long-form, podcast apps, LinkedIn, the newsletter (Substack/Beehiiv discovery), and guest spots on podcasts in that world.
- **Credibility is the price of entry.** This audience checks credentials. Brian is the **guide and translator** who curates experts (Dr. Mirhom, Marianne Girges LMFT, Thrive clinicians, credited authors). He is not the guru. Every claim cites its source.
- **What stays CMH's own:** the Corner (care and belonging), the fighter proof stories and community (CMH Cares, Social Club, ambassadors). That's what Gervais and Brooks *don't* have. It's our edge in their market.

## 3. The CMH Mastery × Meaning lens (every agent runs it)

Before anything ships, check:
1. **Inner game (Gervais).** Does it point attention *inward*, to what the person controls, rather than outward to opinions, scores or likes?
2. **Trainable skill (Gervais).** Is there a rep, a practice or a tool, not just inspiration?
3. **Whole-life meaning (Brooks).** Does it connect performance to enjoyment, satisfaction or meaning, not just winning?
4. **The long arc (Brooks).** Does it respect who the person is beyond this season, this fight or this job?
5. **Science made human (both).** Is there one credited idea, explained plainly, carried by a real story?

If a piece only does 1–2, it's a performance tip. If it only does 3–4, it's a wellness tip. **CMH content does both.**
