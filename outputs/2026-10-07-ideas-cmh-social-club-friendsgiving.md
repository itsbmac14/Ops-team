## 0. The challenge, in one line

**Design an invitation-only evening that makes high performers feel *seen as people*, not worked as prospects, and that becomes the first room where CMH Movement meets the Gervais/Brooks audience face to face.**

- **Audience ring:** Growth Target A (high performers: founders, executives, creators, coaches, athletes, first responders) and Growth Target B (meaning-seekers), seated next to the core community (fighters, ambassadors, the CMH team) and current partners.
- **Pillar:** **Community** (relationships, network, groundedness). Nutrition and Recovery come along quietly through the food, the drinks and the pace of the night.
- **Mastery × Meaning check:** Gervais says the mind is trained in part through *conversations with wise people*. Brooks names *friendship* as one of the pillars of a happy life. A dinner built on those two ideas is a natural CMH format.

## 2. The top 3

1. **#1 The Corner Table.** It's the strongest on fit and teaching value. It's already CMH's language, it carries a usable practice (naming your corner), and it works for a fighter, a founder and a parent equally. Effort M.
2. **#6 The Gratitude Letter.** The highest emotional return for the lowest effort. It reaches people who weren't in the room (the letter recipients), and it's the single moment guests will remember and retell. Effort S.
3. **#10 The Pass-It-On Seat.** It answers "how do we make it feel reserved without making it snobby?" and turns one dinner into a Club. Strong long-term reach with almost no cost. Effort S.

**Recommendation:** Build **The Corner Table** as the concept and fold the other two in as signature moments. Also borrow **#7 (Four Corners seating)** for the seating chart and the **personal philosophy card from #5**. One night, one idea, several outputs.

## 4. Experience design

### 4.1 Name and tagline

**The club:** **CMH Social Club.** Present it like a members' club: a small monogram (**CMH · SC**) and the line *"By invitation."* Never "meetup," "mixer" or "networking."

**The evening (pick one):**
| Option | Tagline | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **The Corner Table** (recommended) | *"A table for the people in your corner."* | Warm, ownable and tied to the brand line |
| The Corner Table | *"An evening of gratitude and good company."* | Quieter and more classic |
| The Gratitude Supper | *"Good food. Real conversation. The people who hold us up."* | Most universal; less CMH-specific |
| The First Table | *"The CMH Social Club, inaugural evening."* | Positions it as night one of a series |

**Invitation line:** *"The CMH Social Club requests the pleasure of your company at The Corner Table, a Friendsgiving evening. [CONFIRM: date] · [CONFIRM: city/venue] · By invitation."*

### 4.2 Guest strategy

**Size:** **20–24 seated** (inside the 15–25 range from the Sponsorship Dinner precedent: personal, not a banquet). One long table if the venue allows it. Everyone at the same table is the whole point.

**The mix (roughly thirds):**
| Third | Who | Examples from the real network |
|---|---|---|
| **The CMH corner** | Hosts, team, ambassadors, practitioners | Anthony, Brian, Andy, Kyle · 1–2 ambassadors (Noel, Mai or Brooke [CONFIRM]) · Dr. Mirhom, Marianne Girges LMFT [CONFIRM] |
| **Current partners** | People already in the CMH network, invited as guests, not as sponsors | Thrive Psychological Services, Motus PT, Landoing Insurance Broker, Rocket Doctor, Alleviate Tax, Manhattan Stitching Company, Fightbook [CONFIRM which, and who from each] |
| **New high performers and meaning-seekers** | Founders, executives, creators, coaches, athletes outside boxing, first responders/military, artists: the Gervais/Brooks audience | [CONFIRM: names, from Anthony, Andy, Tim and Brian's networks] |

**Who sits where:** Use Four Corners seating. Every stretch of the table has someone from the Ring, the Arena, the Studio and the Line. Separate people who arrive together. Never seat a sponsor next to a sponsor or a prospect next to Tim.

**How invitations work:**
1. **Personal first.** A text or call from the person who knows the guest (Anthony, Andy, Tim, Brian or Kyle), *then* the printed invitation. Never a mass email.
2. **The printed card.** Cream stock, black and gold, mailed or hand-delivered [CONFIRM: print budget and lead time]. RSVP by replying to the host personally, not through a form link.
3. **The selection test (internal):** *Do they do meaningful work at a high level? Would the people around them say they show up? Will they give more to the table than they take from it?* Title, net worth and follower count don't qualify anyone. (The same principle as the ambassador program: authenticity over reach.)
4. **Attendance is free** [CONFIRM]. A ticket price turns guests into customers.

**What makes it reserved without being snobby:**
- **Small and specific.** Twenty-some seats, and each guest is told why *they* were invited, in one honest sentence.
- **The gate is character, not status.** A fighter and a CEO receive the same card.
- **First names only.** Place cards and name cards carry first names. No titles and no company names.
- **The table rule: no "what do you do?" until dessert.** Guests meet the person before the résumé. This one rule flattens status faster than anything else.
- **The Pass-It-On Seat.** At the close, each guest gets a card to nominate one person for the next table. The Club grows by trust.

### 4.4 Food and drink direction

**Food:** A Friendsgiving menu cooked with real craft. Seasonal and family-style, generous but not heavy. [CONFIRM: chef/caterer]
- Familiar Thanksgiving touchstones made elegant, alongside a few non-traditional dishes so no one feels it's "someone else's holiday."
- The Nutrition pillar shows up as quality and seasonality, never as preaching. Nobody gets lectured about macros at dinner.
- Dietary needs are collected privately at RSVP. Accommodations arrive plated the same way as everything else, so no one is singled out.
- Optional, light-touch: a single line on the menu naming the pillar each course leans toward. Brian decides whether that's charming or too much.

**Drink: zero-proof as a first-class program, not a substitute.**
- **Two signatures for every pour, presented as equals.** Each course has a spirited pairing and a zero-proof pairing, each with its own name and ingredients. Same glassware, same garnish care and the same price point in spirit. [CONFIRM: beverage partner/bartender]
- **The menu never labels anything "mocktail" or "non-alcoholic."** Both drinks are listed side by side by name and ingredients. Anyone who wants to know can read it.
- **Servers ask the same question of everyone:** *"Would you like the [Name A] or the [Name B]?"* Never *"Are you drinking tonight?"* Brief the staff on this explicitly.
- **Pace:** no open bar. Pairings are poured per course, the last spirited pour comes with dessert, and the nightcap is zero-proof plus coffee and tea. Water is on the table all night.
- **Open decision for Anthony:** consider a fully zero-proof evening. For a mental health organization, that could be the most elegant statement of the night. (See §8.)
- Rides home offered to everyone [CONFIRM].

### 4.5 Look and feel

- **Palette:** black, deep green and cream, with gold accents (CMH palette).
- **Table:** one long table if possible. Deep green or black linen, cream napkins, low candlelight (tapers and votives, nothing that blocks eye contact), low seasonal greenery. The light should flatter faces, because faces are the point.
- **Paper:** cream stock with black type and a touch of gold foil or ink on the monogram [CONFIRM: print budget]. Invitation, place cards (first names), menu, three round cards, letter card, philosophy card and Pass-It-On card are one family of print.
- **Branding:** monogram only. No banners, step-and-repeat, logo projections or sponsor signage. If there's a presenting partner, one quiet line on the menu: *"With thanks to [CONFIRM: partner]."*
- **Sound:** low, warm music during arrival and dessert. Quiet enough to hear the person across the table.
- **Dress:** *"Come as you'd dress for a good dinner with friends."* Elevated, not black tie.

### 4.6 Keepsake

**The Corner Card set**, in a small cream envelope sealed with the monogram:
1. The three round questions, so the conversation can travel home.
2. The guest's own personal philosophy card, written at the table.
3. Their portrait from the Threshold, **delivered a week later** as a print with a handwritten note [CONFIRM: print format]. A second touchpoint that is pure care.

*Optional upgrade [CONFIRM]:* a small stitched item (a napkin or pouch with the CMH · SC monogram), explored with Manhattan Stitching Company, CMH's gear partner. Only if it's genuinely beautiful and the cost is confirmed.

## 5. The Pitch to the Team (vision memo)

**To:** Anthony, Kyle, Andy, Pamela, Tim, Mikko
**From:** Brian
**Re:** The Corner Table: the first CMH Social Club evening

Team,

Here's something most people never think about. The people who perform at the highest level, the founders, the fighters, the coaches, the first responders, spend most of their time being asked what they produce. Almost nobody asks them who holds them up. And the people who hold them up rarely hear a thank-you at the table.

CMH3 showed what this team can build. We put 28 fighters on UFC Fight Pass. The CMH Boxing account crossed 9.55M views in 90 days, 92% from people who didn't follow us yet. Anthony put the Social Club in his 9/30 notes, and Pamela already owns the logistics for it in the ambassador deck. The idea is already ours. What I'm adding is a first night.

It's not a fundraiser. It's not a networking dinner. **It's a Friendsgiving for the people in our corner, and for the people we want in it.**

**The Corner Table** is twenty-some guests at one long, candlelit table the week before Thanksgiving. A third are the CMH family, a third are our current partners, and a third are high performers and meaning-seekers we haven't met yet, from sport, business, the arts and service. Arthur Brooks writes that friendship is one of the pillars of a happy life. Michael Gervais talks about training the mind through conversations with wise people. This evening puts both at one table.

The architecture is simple:
1. **Three rounds, three questions.** One per course. First names only, and no "what do you do?" until dessert.
2. **One fighter, one story.** Two minutes from an ambassador on the day they needed a corner.
3. **The Corner Toast.** Anyone who wants to names one person who holds them up.
4. **The Gratitude Letter.** Every guest writes one. We mail them so they land Thanksgiving week.
5. **The Pass-It-On Seat.** Each guest nominates one person for the next table. That's how the Club grows.

No slides, no auction, no ask. Partners come as guests. Follow-ups happen days later, one to one, starting with thank you.

Why us: nobody else in this space has the corner. Gervais and Brooks have the ideas. We have the fighters, the families, the Thrive partnership and a community that already shows up for each other. This dinner lets the audience Anthony wants us to reach feel that in person. One night gives us a newsletter essay, a Skool tool, future podcast guests, an ambassador spotlight and relationships that compound.

Here's the ask: give me a date, a city and a budget envelope. Pamela and I will take it from there with a working plan by [CONFIRM: date].

Every fight needs a corner. Let's set the table.

— Brian
